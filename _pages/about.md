I am a postdoctoral researcher in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (UIUC) in the <a href="https://stillwell.ece.illinois.edu/">Stillwell Power Group</a>. Broadly speaking, my research is in power electronics. These are the electronic circuits responsible for converting power from one format or magnitude to another. Everyday examples include consumer electronics (think your phone charger), electric vehicle chargers, or microinverters for rooftop solar. My research is grounded in a strong interest in solving large-scale electrification challenges with an emphasis on grid-tied and transportation systems. I specialize in developing a better understanding of conducted electromagnetic interference (EMI), which describes frequency-domain power content in power converters. Conducted EMI is a concern in many application spaces as poor EMI can cause failure of electronic systems.

In May 2026 I completed my Ph.D. in <a href="https://eecs.berkeley.edu/">Electrical Engineering and Computer Sciences</a> at the University of California, Berkeley, under the guidance of Professor Robert Pilawa-Podgurski. At Berkeley I had the opportunity to work within the <a href="https://bpec.berkeley.edu/">Berkeley Power and Energy Center</a> on the design and control of high-performance multilevel power converters for single-phase DC-AC/AC-DC systems. I began my research in EMI in the context of modeling and managing EMI in multilevel circuit topologies. My graduate research was generously supported by the NSF Graduate Research Fellowship.

Prior to starting my degree at Berkeley, I graduated summa cum laude with my B.S in Engineering Science and Mathematics from Smith College. There, I was engaged in undergraduate research related to both engineering education and water resources. 

When I'm not tinkering with circuits, I'm an avid hiker and a self-proclaimed slow runner.
